# BLACK INK — Prompt Template

Use the uploaded source photo as the only source for scene structure, person placement, pose, clothing, accessories, lighting, perspective, and environment.

Keep the entire environment photographic and unchanged. Do not redraw or replace architecture, streets, landscape, sky, furniture, vehicles, objects, products, lighting, shadows, perspective, or depth.

Replace only the source person with a black-and-white hand-drawn illustration in the exact same position. Preserve the original action, body orientation, limb direction, hairstyle, clothing silhouette, accessories, shoes, bags, glasses, hats, and handheld objects as closely as possible.

Character style: abstract editorial character, clean black illustration linework, clear hand-drawn quality, dominant pure-white interior fill, crisp structure, minimal gray only where necessary, clean white sticker-like separation from the photo. Preserve the hairstyle through its outer silhouette with only a few structural lines; do not render individual hair strands.

FACIAL RULE — MANDATORY: treat the internal face as an almost blank symbolic surface. Use exactly two small solid-dot eyes, a tiny simple mouth or no mouth, and omit the nose by default. At most, the nose may be one tiny dot or an extremely short mark. Do not draw realistic eye shapes, eyelids, irises, pupils, lashes, eyebrows, ears, nose bridge, nostrils, lip contours, smile creases, cheek curves, jaw modeling, facial planes, portrait shading, or individualized realistic facial features. Keep this same abstraction even when the face is close, turned, or clearly visible. If the source face is hidden, rear-facing, occluded, or too small to read, do not invent a face or facial marks. Recognition must come from pose, hair silhouette, clothing, accessories, and context—not detailed facial likeness.

PROPORTION LOCK — MANDATORY: keep the illustrated head inside the source head's original bounding box; never enlarge it. Match the source shoulder width, torso length, limb length, seated height, age impression, weight distribution, and total occupied area. Keep adult, elderly, and teen bodies faithful to the source. Do not shorten legs, widen the head, round the torso, or convert the subject into a cute mascot. For multiple people, preserve each person's separate scale, pose, spacing, gaze direction, and interaction.

DETAIL BUDGET — MANDATORY: use silhouette-first line economy. Describe each garment with its outer contour, major opening, hem, cuff, and only a few decisive internal structure lines. Do not trace every photographic fold. Reduce hair to a solid or near-solid graphic mass with very few directional marks. Simplify hands and shoes into clean shapes without knuckles, laces, sole tread, knit patterns, fabric texture, stubble dots, or material hatching. Preserve the subject through silhouette, pose, clothing blocks, and accessories—not surface detail.

Avoid dense crosshatching, dirty pencil texture, messy overlapping lines, comic-panel treatment, heavy gray fill, rough sketchbook aesthetics, anime facial construction, portrait sketching, strand-by-strand hair rendering, stubble, dense garment folds, shoe tread, knit detail, lace detail, fabric texture, or technical fashion-sketch rendering.

TEXT MODE:
- CUSTOM COPY: use the user's copy exactly; only design the hierarchy, line breaks, scale, lettering style, and placement.
- AUTO COPY: generate concise copy from the actual environment/activity; typography must visually relate to the place.
- NO TEXT: add no words at all; only optional non-text doodles.

When typography is used, prefer environment-aware white bold brush/block lettering for the main title plus readable medium-small handwritten or editorial notes. The title must remain secondary to the person and environment; decorative copy should be readable, not watermark-small. Avoid generic repeated typography across unrelated scenes.

Do not copy any specific person, pose, clothing, environment, composition, title, or element placement from style references.
