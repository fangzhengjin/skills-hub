# COLOR CHIBI — Prompt Template

Use the uploaded source photo as the only source for scene structure, person placement, pose, clothing, colors, accessories, lighting, perspective, and environment.

Keep the entire environment photographic and unchanged. Do not redraw or replace architecture, streets, landscape, sky, furniture, vehicles, objects, products, lighting, shadows, perspective, or depth.

Replace only the source person with a colorful editorial-chibi character in the exact same position. Preserve the original action logic, body direction, hairstyle, hair color, clothing type, clothing colors, key accessories, shoes, bags, glasses, hats, and handheld objects.

COLOR CHIBI must be abstract but not babyish. The face should be more reduced than the body.

Face rules: use two small dot eyes, a tiny simple mouth, minimal or omitted nose, and optional light blush. Avoid detailed anime eyes, glossy irises, heavy lashes, or realistic eye rendering.

Body rules: do not default to 2–3-head proportions. Adults should usually read around 4.5–6 heads tall, teens around 4–5, children around 3.5–4.5. Head size may be only moderately enlarged. Preserve long legs, slim silhouettes, crouching poses, leaning poses, and fashion-oriented body rhythm whenever present.

Character style: clear friendly silhouette, readable color blocks, controlled simple shading, white sticker border. A light paper-like / colored-pencil / softly printed illustration texture is allowed, but it must remain subtle and clean. Avoid muddy watercolor, thick painterly rendering, rough scribbles, excessive sketch lines, dirty crayon noise, muddy colors, blurry edges, or overly toddler-like body proportions.

TEXT MODE:
- CUSTOM COPY: use the user's copy exactly; only design the hierarchy, line breaks, scale, lettering style, and placement.
- AUTO COPY: generate concise copy from the actual environment/activity; typography must visually relate to the place.
- NO TEXT: add no words at all; only optional non-text doodles.

When typography is used, prefer environment-aware white bold brush/block lettering for the main title plus readable medium-small handwritten or editorial notes. The title must remain secondary to the person and environment; decorative copy should be readable, not watermark-small. Avoid generic repeated typography across unrelated scenes.

Do not copy any specific person, pose, clothing, environment, composition, title, or element placement from style references.
